### Why the dock-derived wheelbase was short

The original See3CAM calibration included two constraints that the left and right shelf sides run perpendicular to its back edge in the ground plane. Both used a 5 mm error scale. Removing only those two terms and retaining the same four glyph corners, ramp lip, shelf-plane height, lens distortion, focal prior and observed hub centers changed the B wheelbase estimates from **41.45 / 41.99 mm** to **48.69 / 49.20 mm**. The two B hub pairs contributed no observations to either camera fit. The 48 mm reference is used only for scoring and the projected overlay.

The 5-point dock reprojection RMS fell from **0.876 to 0.611 native pixels** while the fitted camera height rose from 132.1 to 166.0 mm. A distant, almost planar set of dock points admits materially different camera poses with similar pixel errors. The extra rectangular-shelf assumption selected the pose that compressed the nearby metric wheelbase. This establishes the cause of the short estimate within the old fit. It does not determine whether the modeled right angles are physically inaccurate, the shelf corners were picked imperfectly, or the lens model extrapolates poorly at those side boundaries.

In the overlay, each rear rim uses its 9.25 mm STL radius and a fixed 48 mm fore-aft span. Its front center is anchored to the observed front center. The side-plane direction is estimated from the two observed centers at their reference heights. The overlay tests the longitudinal length at that measured heading. Dock fit residuals are in-sample. Other isolated checks gave small changes. Replacing 94 mm with 90 mm for the lip depth produced 41.39 / 41.97 mm. Refitting the distortion from all seven archived straight edges gave 41.86 / 42.23 mm. Holding the original camera fixed would require lowering the nominal hub heights by 18.49 / 17.08 mm to obtain 48 mm, placing both centers beneath the shelf. The earlier phone fit reused the compressed See3CAM intersections as B calibration targets, so agreement across its cameras was partly shared by construction.
